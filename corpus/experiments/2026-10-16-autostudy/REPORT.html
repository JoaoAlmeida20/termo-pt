A display bug that comes from the word lists

buildAccentMap keeps the last accented spelling for each unaccented key. The current valid list has 140 keys with more than one spelling, so typing AGORA can show ÁGORA, and HOMEM can show HOMÉM. Candidates and features

I wrote an affix expander (hunspell_expand.py) on top of spylls' parser. It reproduces Hunspell's lookup exactly in both directions and records what spylls drops: Natura's morphology for each rule (+N=p for plural, +G=f for feminine, verb tense and person), the lemma's POS, and its pre-AO90 spelling. The pre-AO mapping matters because SUBTLEX-PT and most corpora predate the spelling reform. Without it, ator, atual and setor look rare, since their counts sit under actor, actual and sector.

Familiarity

Familiarity is the mean Zipf frequency over the European-Portuguese sources: SUBTLEX-PT, OpenSubtitles-pt, Leipzig PT news and P-PAL. Two genres (subtitles and newspapers) give a better commonness signal than either alone.

Brazilianism detector

The detector combines three independent signals:

  1. Accent rule (deterministic): ê/ô where European Portuguese writes é/ó, as in tênis→ténis or gênio→génio. It fires when the é/ó form is a known EP spelling, or when the circumflex comes before m/n and the word isn't in the EP dictionary.
  2. Wiktionary: every sense of the word is tagged Brazil, or the word is a form of a Brazil-only lemma (e.g. bebês → bebê). Head labels apply only to their own entry. The first version applied them to the whole word, which wrongly flagged treze and praça.
  3. Frequency ratio: the median of the log₂ BR/EP relative-frequency ratio across subtitles, Leipzig news and AC/DC news.

The ratio threshold comes from a leakage model, not a guess. European-Portuguese subtitles contain about 6.5% Brazilian text: ônibus, celular and trem show up there at 6–10% of their Brazilian rate. Above a ratio of 3.3, leakage alone explains most of a word's EP occurrences, so the word is flagged. Between 1.5 and 3.3 the word is "BR-leaning" and only goes to review. Words like legal, pegar and jeito sit in that band: Brazil uses them more, but they are normal in Portugal too.

Rules for answers

Each rule is either hard (always applies) or soft (applies only when the LLM review did not endorse the word).

RuleTypeWhat it checks
spellinghard / softMust be in pt_PT (AO90). Words outside it but in Wiktionary or pt_BR, and not Brazilian, need familiarity ≥ 3; that part is soft (lótus, islão, sifão).
shapehardMust be a lemma: a dictionary stem of any POS except proper noun, a feminine singular, or a participle used as an adjective. Plurals, conjugated verbs, and words Wiktionary knows only as plural or verb forms (artes, lares, apaga) are rejected. Infinitives are allowed.
homographsoftAlso a conjugated verb, and SUBTLEX's dominant POS is verbal: tinha (ringworm, or a form of ter), chama, cobra. Also covers contractions (daqui, disso) and AO90 lowercase months (abril, verão).
brhard / softThe accent rule and Wiktionary are hard; the frequency ratio is soft.
weirdsoftEvery Wiktionary sense is archaic, obsolete, rare, regional or similar; or the spelling is superseded; or the word is mostly vulgar.
rarehard / softFamiliarity < 2.25 is hard; between 2.25 and 3.0 it is soft, so the review can promote a word.
reviewhardThe LLM verdict must be keep, with a familiarity rating ≥ 3 out of 5.

LLM review

Ten parallel reviewers, each prompted as a European-Portuguese lexicographer, labelled 3,819 words: my candidate pool (familiarity ≥ 2), plus all of your current answers and your ESCOLEX run, so all three lists were judged on equal terms. Each word got a verdict (keep / obscure / br / foreign / vulgar / inflected / proper / error) and a familiarity rating from 1 to 5. Each batch also included 25 words from another batch, to measure consistency.

  • Self-consistency: 93% identical verdicts on 243 duplicated words, and 100% of familiarity ratings within ±1.
  • Gold Brazilian words: 31 of 40 labelled br and 7 labelled inflected (they are BR plurals), so 38 of 40 were rejected.

Valid guesses

A word is valid if it is in pt_PT (LibreOffice or the full Natura list), or it is a Wiktionary word that is not a proper name, not archaic-only, not Brazilian, and has real EP usage (≥ 3 occurrences in SUBTLEX or ≥ 20 in OpenSubtitles-pt). Pre-AO90 spellings (acção, actor, tecto, jacto) are added when their unaccented key is free, since older players still type them. Each unaccented key keeps exactly one spelling: an answer wins first, then an EP spelling, then the more familiar one.

The first version accepted any word VERO knew, which let in lowercased names (laura, jones, timor) and stray foreign tokens from subtitles (gotta, mamma). I caught this by sampling the output and tightened the rule.

7 · Limitations

  • LLM judges share a model family. The reviewers, the blind judges and I are all Claude models. Using Sonnet for one blind judge adds some diversity, and the 90% agreement is reassuring, but a native speaker reading even 200 words would be a stronger check.
  • Some tuning used review labels. Several shape-rule fixes (feminine forms, homographs, contractions) came from inspecting where the rules disagreed with the review. The rules-only 96% is therefore slightly optimistic. The blind A/B test does not have this problem.
  • News ratios pick up proper nouns. folha scores as Brazilian because of Folha de S. Paulo, and cesta because of cesta básica. The median across three sources and the review override contain this, but it's why the frequency ratio is only a soft rule.
  • The valid list leans permissive. It accepts some English loans with real EP use (crash, poker, story), and Natura accepts a handful of Brazilian words (mamãe, bonde). Guesses cost nothing, so I preferred not rejecting real words.
  • Licensing. The output lists are derived data. OpenSubtitles and Wiktionary are CC BY-SA, the dictionaries GPL/LGPL/MPL, and Linguateca, CLUL and Leipzig have informal or unconfirmed terms. The repo is GPL-3.0 and the README already credits SUBTLEX-PT. Add attributions for the new sources before promoting the lists.
  • Small gaps. Plural-only nouns (juros, bodas, férias) and some loans outside Natura (écran, rugby) are rejected as answers even when reviewers liked them.

8 · Next steps

  1. Pick a variant and promote it: copy the two files into corpus/ and src/data/, and extend the README's word-list credits.
  2. Native-speaker pass on the margin. The words most likely to be wrong are easy to pull out: the 102 BR-leaning answers (br_lean=1 in answers-scored.tsv) and the 170 feminine forms. A small review page with saved verdicts would make this a 20-minute job.
  3. Small app fix: in resolveAccented, prefer today's answer when the unaccented guess matches it, so typing LOUCA on a louça day shows LOUÇA.
  4. Other word lengths: the pipeline takes a length parameter in most steps, so 4- and 6-letter lists would mostly be a re-run plus another review pass.
  5. Plural-only nouns: add a short allowlist (férias, óculos, juros, calças).
  6. Fold the useful parts into extract_words.py, or keep the experiment folder as the generator. run_all.sh rebuilds everything in about a minute after fetch_sources.sh.
